# The Rung method

A way for any school to teach every child from the level they're actually at, in reading and maths, without joining a programme.

Version 0.1, October 2026. Draft. Not yet tested with children.

---

## Why this exists

In a typical grade 4 class in a low- or middle-income country, some children read stories and others can't yet name a letter. Teaching them all from the grade 4 textbook leaves most of them behind. Seven in ten ten-year-olds in these countries can't read and understand a simple text ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/education/brief/foundational-learning)).

The fix is well known: check each child, group children by level rather than grade, and teach each group at its level for part of every day. Pratham's Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) is the best-tested version of this. Randomised trials in India and Africa have found large gains ([J-PAL evidence review](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/policy-insight/tailoring-instruction-students-learning-levels-increase-learning)), and the World Bank's evidence panel rates it a "great buy" ([GEEAP 2023](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2023/05/09/education-smart-buys-cost-effectively-supporting-teachers-and-parents-can-lead-to-significant-learning-improvements)).

TaRL usually reaches a school as a programme, with trainers, mentors and materials from a partner organisation. Most of the world's schools will never be part of one. The Rung method writes the same principles down as an open set of rules that a school can run by itself, with a free phone tool that does the checking, grouping and planning.

**Rung is not TaRL** and has no connection to Pratham, J-PAL or TaRL Africa. It borrows their published principles. If your school can join a TaRL programme, do: trained mentors are the most proven route. Rung is for the schools that can't.

---

## The method in one paragraph

Pick a **ladder**: one skill, such as reading or arithmetic, broken into four to six rungs. **Check** each child one at a time, in about a minute, and place them on the highest rung they pass. **Group** the class by rung. **Teach** each group at its rung in a protected block every school day. **Recheck** every two weeks and move children up. When a child reaches the top rung, they're done with the ladder.

---

## Fixed and flexible

The evidence for this approach comes from a few specific design choices. Change those and there's no longer a reason to expect it to work. Everything else is yours to adapt.

**Fixed (the rules)**

1. A ladder has 4 to 6 rungs. Each rung is a task a child does aloud or on paper, with a pass rule that two teachers would score the same way.
2. Every child is checked individually, not with a written class test.
3. Children are grouped by rung, never by grade or age. A group has one rung.
4. Teaching happens in a protected daily block of at least 30 minutes, ideally an hour, on the timetable every school day.
5. In the block, each group works on activities for its own rung. Children spend more time doing (speaking, reading, solving) than listening.
6. Every child is rechecked at least every 10 school days. A child moves up as soon as they pass the next rung.
7. A child who passes the top rung leaves the block and goes back to grade-level work.

**Flexible (yours to change)**

- The language, scripts and examples in the check cards and lessons.
- Which ladders you run. Start with one.
- Who leads a group: the teacher, a second teacher, a volunteer or an older student.
- When the block happens and how long it is, above 30 minutes.
- Whether the check is done on a phone, on paper cards, or imported from an assessment you already run.
- Materials. Every activity in the library needs only chalk, paper and things found in a classroom.

## 1. Ladders

A ladder is ordered: a child who passes a rung should also pass every rung below it. Rung 0 is always "Beginning", for children who don't yet pass rung 1. It has no check of its own.

The two starter ladders are adapted from the reading and arithmetic tasks used by [ASER Centre](https://asercentre.org/) and TaRL.

### Reading

Works for alphabetic scripts (such as English) and alphasyllabic ones (such as Hindi). In alphasyllabic scripts, "letters" means *akshar*.

| Rung | Name | Check | Pass rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Beginning | — | Doesn't pass Letters |
| 1 | Letters | Name 5 letters shown in random order | 4 of 5 correct |
| 2 | Words | Read 5 common, short words | 4 of 5 correct |
| 3 | Paragraph | Read 4 simple sentences (grade 1 level) | Reads them as sentences, not word by word, with 3 mistakes or fewer |
| 4 | Story | Read an 8–10 sentence story (grade 2 level) | Reads it as sentences with 3 mistakes or fewer |

The check starts at Paragraph.

### Arithmetic

| Rung | Name | Check | Pass rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Beginning | — | Doesn't pass Numbers 1–9 |
| 1 | Numbers 1–9 | Name 5 single-digit numbers | 4 of 5 correct |
| 2 | Numbers 10–99 | Name 5 two-digit numbers | 4 of 5 correct |
| 3 | Subtraction | Two 2-digit subtractions with borrowing, on paper | Both correct |
| 4 | Division | One 3-digit by 1-digit division, on paper | Correct |

The check starts at Numbers 10–99.

### Writing a new ladder

Anyone can write a ladder for another language or foundational skill. To count as a Rung ladder it must:

1. Have 4 to 6 rungs, ordered, with a pass rule for each.
2. Have at least three parallel versions of every check card, so a recheck isn't a memory test.
3. Have each check take under a minute for most children.
4. Be reviewed by at least two teachers who speak the language, and tried with at least 30 children, before it's used to group a class. Record where children got stuck and whether the order held.
5. Come with at least three lesson plans for every rung.

## 2. The check

One child, one teacher, about a minute, somewhere the rest of the class can't hear.

1. Start at the ladder's starting rung.
2. If the child passes, check the next rung up. Keep going until they don't pass, or they pass the top rung.
3. If the child doesn't pass the starting rung, check the rung below. Keep going down until they pass, or reach Beginning.
4. Place the child on the highest rung they passed.

In a recheck, start one rung above the child's current rung and only go up. A child who doesn't pass stays where they are.

Use a different version of each card each round. Be warm and quick. Don't correct the child during the check, and never show children each other's results.

A whole class of 40 takes about an hour, which can be spread over a few days.

---

## 3. Groups

- One group per rung that has children on it. Usually that means four or five groups.
- If a group is large (more than about 15), split it into two groups at the same rung.
- If the timetable allows, combine classes in the same grade band (for example grades 3 to 5) and group across them. This lets one teacher take one rung.
- Sit each group together, so the teacher can move between them.

---

## 4. The daily block

A 45-minute block usually has three parts:

| Part | Time | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Together | ~10 min | The whole group, led by the adult: a song, a game, reading or counting aloud |
| In small groups | ~25 min | Groups of 4–5 children doing an activity, with the adult moving between them |
| On their own | ~10 min | Each child writes, reads or solves something by themselves |

Each rung has a set of day plans in this shape. The tool rotates through them and starts again after a recheck. Lessons use only chalk, paper, cards made from paper, and objects found in a classroom such as sticks, stones and bottle caps.

---

## 5. Recheck and move up

- Recheck every child at least every 10 school days, starting one rung above where they are.
- Move children up as soon as they pass. Children are never moved down because of one bad day: if a child fails a rung they had passed before, they stay where they are and are checked again next round.
- The tool shows the head teacher how many children are on each rung after every round. That chart is the measure of whether the block is working.

---

## 6. Children's data

- A child's record is a first name (or a number) and a list of check dates and rungs. Nothing else.
- In the prototype, all of this stays on the teacher's phone and is never sent anywhere.
- Any future version that syncs data will need the school's agreement and parents' consent first, and will never store children's voices, faces or full names.

---

## What we don't know yet

- Whether teachers can run the method from written rules and a phone tool alone, without the mentoring that TaRL programmes provide. This is the central question.
- Whether the new ladders (and the Hindi materials in particular) are ordered correctly and fair to children. They need review by native speakers and trial with children.
- How much of the gain from TaRL survives when the block is shorter, or when it is led by one teacher with a large class.

The plan to find out: interview teachers inside and outside TaRL programmes (late 2026), test the ladders against trained assessors, then run a pilot of about 20 schools compared with 20 (spring 2027) and publish the results whatever they show.
