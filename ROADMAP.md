# Rung roadmap: v0 to v50

From a website and a method to a world where no child grows up unable to read, and where learning is guaranteed for everyone, for life.

Read this honestly. v0 to v12 is a plan. v13 to v30 is a direction that today's technology could reach with enough evidence, money and partners. v31 to v40 needs hardware and AI that are close but not yet cheap or reliable enough. v41 to v50 needs advances that don't exist yet, in technology and in how school systems are organised. Each era starts only once the one before it has evidence that it works.

**We are at v2.**

---

## The target

SDG 4 asks for inclusive, equitable, quality education and lifelong learning for all by 2030. The world will miss it. About 70% of ten-year-olds in low- and middle-income countries can't read a simple text ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/education/brief/foundational-learning)). About 250 million children aren't in school at all ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/gem-report/en)). Hundreds of millions of adults can't read.

Rung's bet is that the fix isn't a new kind of school. It's one loop, run everywhere: **find where each person is, teach them from there, check again**. Today that loop needs a skilled teacher with time, materials and a mentor. Each era below removes one more of those requirements, until the loop runs for anyone, anywhere, at almost no cost.

## Era I: The method (v0–v5)

*Goal: a method a teacher can run from a phone alone, that we have reason to believe works.*

- **v0, Website.** The idea, the evidence, the honest status. *Done.*
- **v1, The method and the first tool.** Rules any school can run, written down and open (CC BY). A prototype for reading and arithmetic: check, group, daily lessons, recheck. *Done.*
- **v2, Profiles.** Comprehension and writing ladders, so the tool sees the spread in classes where everyone can read. AI reads a photo of a child's handwritten page and suggests a rung, a strength and a next step. Each child has a rung on every ladder. *Done. We are here.*
- **v3, Teachers rewrite it.** Twenty or more teachers, inside and outside TaRL programmes, use the prototype and are interviewed. The method changes to match what they say.
- **v4, Validation.** Ladders checked against trained assessors. AI marking compared with experienced teachers on a thousand real pages, including children writing in a second language. Agreement rates published, including where it fails.
- **v5, The generator.** Give it a language and a rung; it produces ladders, check cards, simple stories that only use what a child has learned, and lesson plans. Native-speaking teachers review everything before a child sees it. This turns "English and Hindi" into "any language with a reviewer".

**What must become true:** teachers say they'd use it; the ladders sort children the way experts do; AI marking agrees with teachers often enough to be useful and is fair across groups.

## Era II: Proof (v6–v12)

*Goal: a randomised trial showing children learn more, and one government that runs it themselves.*

- **v6, The app.** Android, offline-first, works on a shared low-end phone. Syncs to a school view only with the school's agreement and parents' consent.
- **v7, The coach.** The mentor TaRL relies on, rebuilt as a daily check-in over WhatsApp or a voice call in the teacher's language. It asks how the block went, answers questions, notices when the block is being skipped, and adjusts the plan.
- **v8, The voice check.** Partner with teams like Wadhwani AI so a child reads aloud to the phone and the rung is scored automatically. Checking a class goes from an hour to a few minutes.
- **v9, The pilot.** About 20 schools using Rung against 20 that don't, preregistered, for a full term. Results published whatever they show.
- **v10, The head teacher's view.** Rungs over time for every class and school. For a district, it's the cheapest real-time learning data it has ever had.
- **v11, Home practice.** A weekly voice call to a parent's basic phone: the child's rung, one game to play, a story to read together.
- **v12, First government.** A state or national system runs Rung in its own schools, on its own budget, with its own staff.

**What must become true:** the pilot shows a real gain, roughly comparable with mentored TaRL; the coach keeps teachers running the block without human visits; the cost per child fits a government budget.

## Era III: Every language (v13–v20)

*Goal: Rung works for any child, in the language they think in.*

- **v13, A hundred languages.** Reviewed ladders and libraries for the hundred most-used languages of instruction.
- **v14, Speech recognition for every child.** Recognition of children's speech for low-resource languages, trained only on consented data and running on the phone.
- **v15, A child's first hundred books.** A free, levelled, printable library in every supported language, each book written for a rung.
- **v16, Mother-tongue bridges.** Children start in their home language and move deliberately to the school language. Ladders show progress in both.
- **v17, Open ladders.** Teachers anywhere write and test ladders. A public registry keeps each ladder's evidence next to it.
- **v18, The early years.** Ladders for ages 3 to 6: talking, listening, number sense, early reading. Built for parents and pre-schools.
- **v19, Every child included.** Checks and ladders that work for children who are deaf, blind or have learning differences. Patterns in the check data flag possible dyslexia, or vision and hearing problems, for referral.
- **v20, A million classrooms.**

## Era IV: The learning engine (v21–v30)

*Goal: the method improves itself from evidence, and covers what children need beyond the basics.*

- **v21, Every lesson is an experiment.** With consent and independent ethics oversight, groups at the same rung get different activities, and the system measures which ones move children up. The largest education trial ever run, running continuously.
- **v22, The method rewrites itself.** The lesson library is ranked by measured effect for each rung, language and setting. Activities that don't work are retired; new ones are tested.
- **v23, Tests disappear.** Rungs update from ordinary classwork: a photo of a page of exercises, a minute of reading aloud, the board at the end of a lesson. Checks stop being events.
- **v24, Early warning.** The system sees a child about to stall weeks before it shows, and tells the teacher what to try.
- **v25, New ladders.** Scientific reasoning, data and chance, digital literacy, civic argument, and social and emotional skills.
- **v26, Teacher ladders.** Teachers get their own profile, measured by how their children climb, with coaching aimed at exactly what they need. It counts toward their professional development.
- **v27, Curricula from evidence.** Ministries set the pace of the national curriculum from what children can actually do, not what a textbook assumes.
- **v28, Through secondary.** Ladders up to age 16: algebra, extended writing, argument, research.
- **v29, Adults.** The same ladders, rewritten for adults who never learned to read, through work, phones and community groups.
- **v30, Ten million classrooms.** Most low- and middle-income countries.

## Era V: Beyond the school (v31–v40)

*Goal: learning reaches people school doesn't, and stops depending on a building or a qualified teacher.*

- **v31, Children out of school.** Learning by voice on any phone and community radio, with a local volunteer as guide and the same ladders as school.
- **v32, Learning that survives a crisis.** When schools close for war, floods or epidemics, learning moves to phones and radio within a day, at each child's rung.
- **v33, The learning passport.** A record of every rung a person has reached that belongs to them, goes where they go, and is recognised across borders. A refugee child starts at their rung, not at the beginning.
- **v34, The ten-dollar slate.** A solar-powered e-ink device with an on-device AI tutor, voice in and out, no connection needed, built to last five years.
- **v35, A tutor for every child.** Twenty minutes a day with a patient tutor at the child's rung on every ladder, alongside the teacher, never instead of one.
- **v36, Where there are no teachers.** A trained local adult and Rung reliably deliver foundational learning where qualified teachers don't exist, proven by trials.
- **v37, Whole families learn.** Parents climb their own ladders alongside their children, and literacy spreads through households instead of one child at a time.
- **v38, Health and learning together.** Checks flag vision, hearing, nutrition and developmental needs and connect families to health services.
- **v39, Under a dollar.** Foundational learning for a child for a year costs less than a dollar beyond the cost of school.
- **v40, Every child on Earth has access.**

## Era VI: Beyond what's possible today (v41–v50)

*Goal: education that is currently impossible, technologically and logistically.*

- **v41, A science of learning at planetary scale.** A privacy-preserving, openly governed record of how a billion people learn. AI research systems propose new ways to teach, test them in consented trials, and publish everything.
- **v42, Tutors that understand minds.** Not just which rung a child is on, but what they believe and why: the particular wrong idea behind a mistake, fixed at its root.
- **v43, The world as the curriculum.** A child's tutor turns their own place, whether the market, the farm, the river or the city, into problems worth solving. Real projects are assessed on the ladders.
- **v44, The end of grades.** School stops sorting children by birthday. Every child moves continuously up every ladder, and schools organise around rungs, interests and projects.
- **v45, Every language, including unwritten ones.** Learning in languages that have no writing system or almost no materials, helping keep endangered languages alive as children learn in them.
- **v46, Motivation understood.** Knowing why each child stops trying, whether that's boredom, fear, hunger or something at home, and responding, so curiosity survives childhood.
- **v47, Teachers freed.** Machines do the measuring, planning, marking and practice. Teachers spend their time on what only people can do: relationships, judgement, encouragement, mentoring.
- **v48, Measured, not estimated.** Every country knows, continuously and accurately, how many of its children can read and calculate. SDG 4.1 is reported from real data, not surveys every few years.
- **v49, Zero learning poverty.** No child anywhere reaches the age of ten unable to read and understand a simple story.
- **v50, The ladder never ends.** Every person on Earth, from their first words to old age, in any language, anywhere, has a companion that knows where they are and what to learn next, alongside the people who teach them, at almost no cost. Learning becomes a guaranteed public good, like clean water. SDG 4 is met.

## What Rung will never be

The bigger it gets, the more these matter.

- **Never a replacement for the teacher-child relationship.** Every version keeps a person responsible for a child's learning.
- **Never surveillance.** Minimal data, consent first, no faces or voices kept, no selling or sharing data for profit, and children never ranked publicly.
- **Never closed.** The method, ladders, lessons and code stay open. Governments must be able to run Rung without depending on us.
- **Never untested.** No version is promoted beyond the evidence for it, and results are published when they're bad.
- **Never only in rich languages.** A feature isn't finished until it works in a low-resource language on a cheap phone.
