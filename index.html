One class. Five rungs. A lesson for each.

In most grade 4 classrooms, some children are reading stories while others can’t yet name a letter. Rung takes a reading check, sorts the class into level groups, and hands the teacher a short lesson for every group, every day.

The boy went to the market to buy rice.

7 in 10 ten-year-olds in low- and middle-income countries can’t read and understand a simple sentence like this.1

We know what works. Teach children at the level they’re actually on, not the grade they’re in, for an hour a day. The World Bank’s evidence panel calls it a “great buy”, one of the most cost-effective ways known to raise learning.3

It stalls in ordinary schools because of everything around the teaching. Someone has to check every child one by one, regroup the class, plan a lesson for each group, and support a teacher who has fifty children and one period. That planning is what Rung takes on.

How it works

  1. Start from a reading check

    Each child reads a short card aloud. Voice-based checks already run at scale, such as Wadhwani AI’s Vaachan Samiksha in Gujarat.4 Rung is designed to start from checks like these, or run a simple one of its own.

  2. Group the class by rung

    Rung sorts children into the five level groups and shows the teacher who sits where. Children move up as soon as they’re ready.

  3. Teach today’s lesson for each group

    Every morning the teacher gets a short, concrete plan for each group, using games, cards and reading activities that need nothing more than chalk and paper. A coach answers questions over a basic phone, in the teacher’s language.

  4. Practise at home

    A short weekly voice call lets a child practise with a parent listening in. It works on any phone, with no data plan or app.

AI has made this kind of support cheap. In a World Bank trial in Nigeria, six weeks of AI-assisted tutoring, with a teacher in the room, matched an estimated 1.5 to 2 years of ordinary schooling.5 The reading check can also flag children who may simply need glasses. A child with poor vision learns about half as much.6

Rung keeps a child’s level. It never keeps a child’s voice.

Our promise about children’s voices

  • Recordings are scored on the phone where possible and deleted after scoring.
  • We keep a child’s level, never their voice, face or full name.
  • Nothing is collected without a school’s agreement and a parent’s consent.
  • The tool is open source, and pilot results are published whether they’re good or bad.

Where we are

Rung is pre-pilot. It hasn’t been tested with children yet. This is the plan for finding out whether it works.

  1. Late 2026Build the grouping and daily lesson plans in English and Hindi. Check the groups against trained human assessors.we are here
  2. Early 2027Add teacher coaching and home practice. Sign a school partner and get ethics approval.
  3. Spring 2027Pilot in about 20 schools, compared with 20 that don’t use Rung, over 50 teaching days.
  4. Late 2027Publish the results, open-source the tool, add languages, and work toward adoption by a state school system.
