One class. Five rungs. A lesson for each.

In most grade 4 classrooms, some children are reading stories while others can’t yet name a letter. Rung is a free method and phone tool any school can run: check each child in a minute, sort the class into level groups, and get a short lesson for every group, every day. From first letters and numbers to confident writing.

The boy went to the market to buy rice.

7 in 10 ten-year-olds in low- and middle-income countries can’t read and understand a simple sentence like this.1

We know what works. Teach children at the level they’re actually on, not the grade they’re in, for an hour a day. The World Bank’s evidence panel calls it a “great buy”, one of the most cost-effective ways known to raise learning.3 Pratham’s version, Teaching at the Right Level, has raised learning in randomised trials in India and Africa.5

But it usually reaches a school as a programme, with trainers and mentors from a partner organisation, and most schools will never be in one. On their own, a teacher with fifty children and one period has to check every child, regroup the class and plan a lesson for each group. Rung writes the method down as open rules any school can follow, and gives the teacher a phone tool that does the checking, grouping and planning.

The Rung method

Pick your ladders: skills like reading, arithmetic, comprehension and writing, each broken into five rungs. Every child gets a rung on every ladder. Then, every two weeks:

  1. Check each child

    One child at a time, about a minute each, on the teacher’s phone or with paper cards. Start in the middle of the ladder, go up while they pass, and place them on the highest rung they pass. For writing, the teacher photographs each child’s page, and AI suggests the rung, a strength and one next step. Results from checks that already run, like Wadhwani AI’s Vaachan Samiksha, could feed in instead.4

  2. Group the class by rung

    Children sit with others on the same rung, whatever their grade. Usually that makes four or five groups.

  3. Teach today’s lesson for each group

    A protected block of 30 to 60 minutes every school day. Each group gets a plan for its rung: together, then in small groups, then on their own. Every activity needs only chalk, paper and things in the classroom.

  4. Recheck and move up

    Every ten school days, recheck and move children up the moment they’re ready. A child on the top rung goes back to grade-level work.

A few rules are fixed, because the evidence rests on them: individual checks, groups by level rather than grade, a daily block, regular rechecks. Everything else, from the language to who leads a group, is yours to change. Anyone can write a ladder for a new language or skill. The method is written up in full and free to use and adapt under CC BY 4.0. The strongest evidence is for foundational reading and maths; the comprehension and writing ladders are an extension we still have to test. Rung isn’t Teaching at the Right Level and has no connection to Pratham. If your school can join a TaRL programme, do.

Try the prototype

Rung keeps a child’s level. It never keeps a child’s voice.

Our promise about children’s voices

  • Recordings are scored on the phone where possible and deleted after scoring.
  • We keep a child’s level, never their voice, face or full name.
  • Nothing is collected without a school’s agreement and a parent’s consent.
  • The prototype keeps everything on the teacher’s phone. The one exception is AI marking, if a teacher turns it on: the photo of a page is read, not kept, and the child’s name is never sent.
  • The tool is open source, and pilot results are published whether they’re good or bad.

Where we are

Rung is pre-pilot. It hasn’t been tested with children yet. This is the plan for finding out whether it works.

  1. Late 2026Publish the method and a working prototype: reading, arithmetic, comprehension and writing, with AI reading of handwritten pages. Interview teachers inside and outside TaRL programmes, and check the ladders and the AI marking against trained assessors.we are here
  2. Early 2027Lessons adapted by AI to each language and classroom, coaching over a basic phone, and home practice. Sign school partners and get ethics approval.
  3. Spring 2027Pilot in about 20 schools, compared with 20 that don’t use Rung, over 50 teaching days.
  4. Late 2027Publish the results, add ladders and languages, and work toward adoption by a state school system.

This is the next year. The full roadmap runs from here, v2, to v50: learning for every person, in any language, for life.
